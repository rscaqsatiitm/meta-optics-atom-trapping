# Campaign 1: static meta-hologram (B1), meta-atom library and high-power thermal model

Technical report, Phase A modelling for ANRF/ARG/2026/009937. All numbers below are
from simulations executed in this repository (type SIM, config and results directory
given) unless marked otherwise; every SIM value should be reviewed before it is quoted
in a submission.

## Summary of results

(i) A 48 x 48 Cs array (2,304 sites, 4.5 um pitch) at 1075 nm can be generated by one
SiN metasurface of 18.4 mm diameter at NA 0.55 through a 4 mm fused-silica cell wall,
with a simulated depth non-uniformity of 4.6 % rms (weakest site 0.83 of the mean)
after two rounds of feedback. This is the 5 mm full-pixel bench value; the in-cell pupil
model gives 0.3 %. Target: <= 10 % rms.

(ii) The standard FFT (Debye) hologram design fails off-axis for direct projection: in the
cell geometry the Strehl ratio falls to 0.94 at 150 um and 0.35 at 300 um from the axis,
with up to 5 um of field curvature. A design with exact per-site optical paths removes
this at no extra cost and is required for the 10^4-site goal.

(iii) A hard-edged phase-only aperture produces a zero-order focus on the optical axis
carrying ~1e-3 of the power, which a sampled design cannot cancel and which outshines a
site for arrays above ~1,000 sites. A soft illumination edge over 10 % of the radius
suppresses it ~30x.

(iv) Meta-atoms: Si-rich SiN pillars (0.50 um pitch, 1.5 um tall, 150 to 390 nm wide) give a
16-level efficiency of 0.96 in the local-periodic approximation, robust to +/-10 nm width
bias (<= 1 % loss). The film index is the dominant tolerance (1 to 2 % per 0.05). At
the large deflections of the outer aperture, coupling between dissimilar neighbours
reduces efficiency to 0.75 to 0.88 (converged supercell RCWA), an aperture-averaged
factor of 0.886.

(v) Power: the model delivers 0.74 of the laser power into ideal foci (2.9 to 3.1 mK for
2,304 sites at 50 W), about twice the utilisation the proposal assumes from ref. 4. The
proposal's site counts are therefore conservative.

(vi) High power (H2a): with the die heat-sunk at its rim, substrate heating produces an
almost pure focal shift (refocused Strehl > 0.9999), -0.18 um per 100 W per 0.1 % film
absorption on 0.5 mm fused silica, ~7x less on sapphire. Explaining the 19 % deficit of
ref. 4 thermally requires ~1.2 % film absorption, so the proposal's thermal hypothesis is
plausible only for strongly absorbing films and should be tested by measuring the
absorption directly.

The main limitations are scalar optics (~5 to 10 % on absolute depths), the
local-periodic treatment of meta-atom errors in the uniformity figures, and full-pixel
validation only at 5 mm aperture (Section 6).

## 1. Scope and method

We model three things: (i) the meta-atom library for square SiN pillars on fused silica
at 1075, 1064.4, 895 and 852 nm (RCWA, fmmax, Jones-direct-Fourier formulation); (ii)
the direct-projection hologram that generates the static Cs array at 1075 nm through the
glass-cell wall (pupil-domain weighted Gerchberg-Saxton design, validated by exact
scalar angular-spectrum propagation of the full 10^8-pixel metasurface at 5 mm
aperture); (iii) steady-state heating of the metasurface substrate at 1 to 100 W and its
effect on the tweezers (axisymmetric finite volume plus a pupil wavefront analysis).
Equations, assumptions and the constants ledger are in `docs/model_notes.md`.

Assumed geometry (all `[CONFIRM?]`): 2.5 mm air, 4 mm fused-silica cell wall, 9 mm
vacuum to the atoms (modelled on ref. 4); metasurface on a 0.5 mm fused-silica wafer
diced to a 25 mm die, held at its rim; 48 x 48 square array at 4.5 um pitch; NA 0.55,
which needs an 18.4 mm aperture in this geometry (19.9 mm for NA 0.58, consistent with
the 19.8 mm of ref. 4).

## 2. Atomic model and power budget

The two-line polarisability model with Steck's matrix elements and measured static
polarisability gives alpha(1064 nm) = 1171 a.u. and alpha(1075 nm) = 1125 a.u., against
the proposal's 1,160 and 1,120 a.u. from ref. 36 (0.9 % and 0.4 % higher). Applied to
ref. 4 (Rb, 850 nm, 33 W, 18,225 sites, 37 % utilisation) with NA_eff = 0.467 derived
from the measured 1.11 um first dark radius, it gives 0.306 mK against the measured
0.305(17) mK. With the proposal's parameters (37 % utilisation, NA_eff 0.47) 50 W at
1075 nm supports 2,809 sites at 1 mK and 9,362 at 0.30 mK. The proposal's "~9,200 at
0.3 mK" corresponds to 0.305 mK; at 0.300 mK the figure is ~9,360. A transport tweezer
at 1.2 mK and 1064.4 nm needs 6.58 mW at NA 0.5 and 7.45 mW at NA 0.47 (proposal: 6.6
and 7.5 mW). (`tests/test_atoms.py`)

## 3. Meta-atom library

Numerics (results/rcwa_convergence, SIM). With the Jones-direct-Fourier formulation the
relative transmission phase converges to 0.003 rad at 197 Fourier terms. The
permittivity grid adds 0.01 to 0.03 rad between 256 and 1024 points, largest for
gaps below 60 nm, which the fabrication limits exclude. The plain-FFT formulation
converges about ten times more slowly (0.07 rad error at 317 terms), and grcwa, which
uses it, agrees with fmmax-FFT to 1e-4 rad at 437 terms. This is an independent check of
the solver wrapper and its phase convention. The library (data/library_main.h5,
configs/library_main.yaml) covers 5 pitches (0.40 to 0.60 um), 13 heights (0.8 to
2.0 um), 40 widths and 4 wavelengths for LPCVD SiN (Luke et al. dispersion) and Si-rich
SiN (n = 2.3, `[VALUE?]`), at 197 terms on a 256 grid.

Selection at 1075 nm (results/library_analysis_1075). Geometries are ranked by the
first-order efficiency of a 16-level blazed grating in the local-periodic approximation,
including the Fresnel loss at the exit face, averaged over a uniform width bias of -10,
0 and +10 nm. Fabrication limits are a minimum feature and gap of 100 nm and h/w <= 10
(`[CONFIRM?: CNNP etch capability]`). About 65 geometries per film are feasible, and the
best dozen lie within 0.5 % of each other, limited by the aspect ratio. Among those within
0.3 % of the best we take the shortest pillar:

| Film | Pitch (um) | Height (um) | Widths (nm) | eta_16 | eta_16, +/-10 nm averaged | Phase coverage (rad) | Min. T0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LPCVD SiN | 0.60 | 1.6 | 168 to 492 | 0.967 | 0.960 | 6.13 | 0.93 |
| Si-rich SiN | 0.50 | 1.5 | 150 to 390 | 0.960 | 0.957 | 6.60 | 0.90 |

The Si-rich height agrees with the proposal's estimate (1.15 um at 852 nm from ref. 14,
~26 % taller at 1075 nm, i.e. ~1.45 um). An earlier ranking with cubic interpolation
returned eta > 1 for resonant geometries (minimum T0 of 0.02 to 0.3); this was spline
overshoot across under-sampled resonances, and those geometries also lose most under a
width bias. Linear interpolation and the bias-averaged metric remove them.

Tolerances of the selected families (8 phase levels, direct RCWA; the common phase
offset is removed, since a uniform offset does not affect the hologram):

| Perturbation | LPCVD: differential phase rms (rad) | LPCVD: efficiency ratio | Si-rich: differential phase rms (rad) | Si-rich: efficiency ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| width +10 nm | 0.050 | 0.998 | 0.079 | 0.998 |
| width -10 nm | 0.071 | 0.997 | 0.064 | 0.991 |
| index +0.05 | 0.126 | 0.979 | 0.117 | 0.989 |
| index -0.05 | 0.146 | 0.984 | 0.109 | 0.986 |
| height +50 nm | 0.045 | 0.998 | 0.065 | 1.000 |

A ±0.05 index error costs 1 to 2 % of efficiency, several times more than a 10 nm width
bias or a 50 nm height error. Hence the film index at 1075 nm should be measured by
ellipsometry on each deposition run and fed into the lookup (materials.from_csv), rather
than taken from the literature.

Edge efficiency (results/library_analysis_1075/supercell.json, edge_factor.json). Blazed
supercells of N selected pillars deflect normally incident light into the first order at
sin(theta) = lambda / (N p), which is the local behaviour of the hologram where the lens
deflects by theta. First-order efficiency, mean of x and y polarisation (RCWA; the
local-periodic prediction for the same widths is 0.96 to 0.97 in every case):

| LPCVD, sin(theta) | 0.224 | 0.299 | 0.358 | 0.448 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eta (x, y) | 0.877, 0.860 | 0.886, 0.793 | 0.863, 0.761 | 0.820, 0.762 |

| Si-rich, sin(theta) | 0.269 | 0.358 | 0.430 | 0.538 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eta (x, y) | 0.877, 0.867 | 0.873, 0.862 | 0.811, 0.858 | 0.851, 0.747 |

These efficiencies are converged: at N = 4 (LPCVD) the result moves from 0.820 to 0.817
to 0.821 between 80 and 200 terms per cell and 48 to 96 grid points per cell, and at N = 8
it lies between 0.869 and 0.877. A supercell of four identical pillars reproduces the
single-cell transmission (T0 = 0.9688 against 0.9689), which checks the supercell setup. The 10 to 20 % shortfall relative to the local-periodic
approximation therefore reflects near-field coupling between neighbouring pillars of
very different widths. It appears mostly as reflection (5 to 15 %, against 3 to 4 % for
uniform arrays) and as the -1 and zero orders (2 to 7 %). Averaged over the
aperture with the incident intensity (NA 0.55, in-cell stack), the ratio to the local-periodic
value is 0.842 (top hat), 0.846 (soft edge) and 0.864 (Gaussian, w = R) for LPCVD, and
0.879, 0.886 and 0.900 for Si-rich SiN. With its higher efficiency at the rim and the
shorter etch, the Si-rich family is our baseline. The meta-atom-limited efficiency of the
hologram is then 0.96 x 0.886 = 0.85 before the pupil-model design efficiency.
Optimising widths for the supercell environment rather than for the periodic array
(e.g. by inverse design on 2 to 4 neighbours) is the standard remedy and is not yet
done.

## 4. Hologram design: field aberration of the standard (Debye) design

### 4.1 The Debye design is not adequate beyond ~100 um from the axis

The usual design (weighted Gerchberg-Saxton with an FFT between pupil and focal plane,
as for SLM arrays) assumes that the metasurface phase which focuses to site x_j is the
on-axis lens phase plus a tilt in direction-cosine space. For direct projection
through a 15 mm path this neglects a path difference of order |x_j|^2 / (2 WD) ~ 0.8 um
at 150 um from the axis (DERIVED). Ray-traced through the actual layer stack
(hologram/field_model.py), the residual after removing tilt and defocus gives, for
NA 0.55 and a top-hat beam (results/hologram_b1_cell20mm, aberration_*.png):

| Distance from axis (um) | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 | 300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strehl ratio, Debye design | 0.999 | 0.988 | 0.939 | 0.819 | 0.346 |
| Axial focus shift (um) | -0.14 | -0.57 | -1.28 | -2.27 | -5.10 |

A 48 x 48 array at 4.5 um pitch has its corners at 150 um, so the Debye design costs ~6 %
of depth at the corners and has 1.3 um of field curvature, above the 1 um focal
tolerance of the error budget. A 100 x 100 array (corners at 318 um) would have
corner Strehl ratios near 0.3. We do not know how refs. 4 and 14 handled this (the
retrieved text of ref. 4 mentions block-wise FFT propagation), but ref. 4's measured
first dark radius of 1.11 um against the 0.9 um design is consistent in sign with
field-dependent aberration (Speculative).

### 4.2 Exact-kernel design

Replacing the tilt kernel by the exact path difference through the stack,
K_ij = exp(-i k0 [OPL(r_i -> x_j) - OPL0(r_i)]), removes the field dependence at no
extra cost for 2,304 sites (K is a 160,000 x 2,304 complex64 matrix, 16 s for 60 WGS
iterations on 4 CPU cores). For the in-cell geometry, evaluated with the exact kernels:

| Design | Mode efficiency, sum over sites | Depth non-uniformity (rms) | Corner / centre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debye | 0.908 | 4.0 % | 0.82 |
| Exact | 0.928 | 0.41 % | 0.99 |

### 4.3 Full-pixel validation and two effects the pupil model misses

We rendered the designs onto a 5 mm, NA 0.5 metasurface (10,240^2 pixels at 0.5 um,
behind a 1 mm fused-silica plate) and propagated the full aperture with the exact scalar
angular-spectrum method (scripts/validate_fullpixel.py). The plain lens reproduces the
paraxial Airy peak intensity to 0.6 %. This geometry has a short working distance
(4.7 mm), so its field aberration is stronger than in the cell (Strehl 0.62 at 150 um
for the Debye design, against 0.94 in the cell). It is therefore a strict test of the
exact kernels.

The validation exposed two effects, both now handled in the code:

1. Rendering. Bilinear interpolation of the hologram phase from the design pupil grid
   acts as a focal-plane envelope sinc^2(x ds / lambda), which weakened corner sites
   by ~2x. The back-propagated pupil field is now evaluated slightly beyond the NA and
   Fourier-upsampled 8x before rendering (exact.render_grid). Direct per-pixel
   evaluation of the exact site superposition gives the same result to 0.1 %.
2. An edge-generated zero order. A hard-edged, phase-only aperture produces a focus
   on the optical axis carrying ~1e-3 of the power, even though the design places only
   2e-6 there. Resolved by radius, the discrepancy comes entirely from the outermost
   ~5 % of the aperture radius, where the zero-order integrand oscillates at full
   amplitude up to a discontinuity. Neither a finer pupil grid nor edge-coverage
   weighting removes it. A raised-cosine edge on the illumination over 10 % of the
   radius suppresses it 31x, from 0.463 to 0.015 of the mean site peak for a 24 x 24 array
displaced from the axis, and raises the array efficiency from 0.858 to 0.890
(configs/fullpixel_zeroorder_2mm_edge0.yaml and _edge100.yaml). Since the
   zero-order power is fixed while the site power scales as 1/N, a hard-edged aperture
   gives a zero-order spot brighter than a site for N ~ 1,000 or more; a soft-edged
   top-hat shaper is therefore a requirement, not an option.

Final full-pixel results, 48 x 48 sites, soft edge (results/fullpixel_bench5mm_softedge;
configs/fullpixel_bench5mm_softedge.yaml):

| Design | Depth non-uniformity (rms) | Min / mean depth | Mean Strehl | Array efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Debye | 23.6 % | 0.28 | 0.91 | 0.877 |
| Exact | 7.8 % | 0.65 | 0.98 | 0.880 |
| Exact + 2 rounds of model feedback | 4.6 % | 0.83 | 0.98 | 0.887 |

Array efficiency is the power within pitch/2 of each site divided by the incident
power, for an ideal phase-only metasurface (no meta-atom losses). The feedback rounds
rescale the WGS targets by (mean / measured peak)^0.5 using the full-pixel result as
the measurement; experimentally this is camera or atom feedback. The residual 4.6 %
is within the 10 % rms target of Table 1, before meta-atom and fabrication errors.

### 4.4 In-cell design: 48 x 48 sites, NA 0.55, 18.4 mm aperture

Pupil-model results with exact kernels (results/hologram_b1_cell20mm, configs/
hologram_b1_cell20mm.yaml). Mode efficiency is the fraction of the incident power
coupled into ideal, aberration-free foci at the sites. The Gaussian case has w = R
and clips 13.5 %, as in ref. 4:

| Illumination | Design | Mode efficiency | Depth non-uniformity (rms) | Min / mean | Corner axial shift (um) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| top hat | Debye | 0.908 | 4.0 % | 0.80 | -1.27 |
| top hat | exact | 0.928 | <0.01 % | 1.00 | 0 (by construction) |
| soft-edge top hat (10 %) | Debye | 0.908 | 3.1 % | 0.84 | -1.28 |
| soft-edge top hat (10 %) | exact | 0.925 | 0.26 % | 0.99 | 0 |
| Gaussian, w = R | exact | 0.927 | 0.34 % | 0.99 | 0 |

The pupil model does not contain the edge-generated zero order or the residual that
feedback removes on the 5 mm bench (7.8 % before feedback, 4.6 % after); we expect
similar residuals here, so the in-cell uniformity figure to plan with is the bench value.

Power budget. Multiplying the mode efficiency by the efficiency chain (Si-rich family:
LPA 0.960, edge 0.886; AR-coated substrate 0.995 `[VALUE?]`; AR-coated cell 0.99
`[CONFIRM?]`; top-hat shaper 0.95 `[VALUE?]`; product 0.796) gives a delivered fraction
of 0.736 for the soft-edged top hat. The ideal spot of this pupil has the peak intensity
per watt of a uniformly filled NA 0.531 focus. In the language of the proposal's
power model these are a utilisation of 0.736 and NA_eff = 0.531, against the 0.37 and
0.47 that the proposal takes from the measured performance of ref. 4:

| Model | Sites at 1 mK, 50 W | Sites at 0.3 mK, 50 W | Depth of 2,304 sites at 50 W |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal (ref. 4 measured: 0.37, 0.47) | 2,811 | 9,370 | 1.22 mK |
| This model, soft-edged top hat | 7,137 | 23,791 | 3.10 mK |
| x 0.95 (scalar normalisation, Section 6) | 6,780 | 22,601 | 2.94 mK |
| x 0.95 x 0.81 (ref. 4 measured / predicted power) | 5,492 | 18,307 | 2.38 mK |

The model figures are upper bounds for a well-fabricated element. They contain no
fabrication errors, no stitching, and no absorption or scattering beyond the
meta-atom LPA and supercell factors. Ref. 4's own prediction exceeded its measurement by
23 %, which motivates the last row. The proposal's figures, built on ref. 4's measured
performance, are therefore conservative by a factor of ~2 to 2.5 relative to this
model. The 2,000-site target of Table 1 is met with margin at either depth in all rows.
Hence the power budget is not the limiting factor for Objective 2. Trap uniformity is
(feedback-dependent, Section 4.3), and so is the zero-order spot.

## 5. High-power thermal model (hypothesis H2a)

The film absorption a_film (fraction of the incident power absorbed in the SiN layer)
is unknown for our films, so it is the free parameter; all results are linear in
a_film x P. Results for the in-cell geometry at NA 0.55 (results/thermal_h2a,
config configs/thermal_h2a.yaml):

| Substrate | Beam | P (W) | a_film | dT_max (K) | Focal shift (um) | Strehl, refocused | Strehl, fixed plane |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fused silica 0.5 mm | top hat | 50 | 1e-3 | 5.1 | -0.09 | 1.0000 | 0.9993 |
| fused silica 0.5 mm | top hat | 100 | 1e-3 | 10.2 | -0.18 | 1.0000 | 0.9974 |
| fused silica 1.0 mm | top hat | 100 | 1e-3 | 6.7 | -0.24 | 1.0000 | 0.9955 |
| sapphire 0.5 mm | top hat | 100 | 1e-3 | 0.75 | -0.025 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| fused silica 0.5 mm | Gaussian, w = R (13.5 % clipped) | 100 | 1e-3 | 14.6 | -0.33 | 1.0000 | 0.9920 |

Three results follow. First, with the die heat-sunk at its rim the substrate thermal
lens is almost a pure defocus over the NA 0.55 pupil: the Strehl ratio after refocusing
stays above 0.9999 in every case run, so substrate heating does not degrade the spots,
it moves them. Second, the error-budget limit of 1 um focal shift at 100 W is reached
at a_film = 5.4e-3 (0.5 mm fused silica, top hat), 4.1e-3 (1 mm fused silica) and
4.0e-2 (0.5 mm sapphire); for a Gaussian beam with w = R it drops to 3.0e-3 on 0.5 mm
fused silica, because the absorbed power is concentrated near the centre. The sapphire margin is ~7x rather than the 24x ratio of
thermal conductivities for two reasons: face cooling already limits the fused-silica
temperature, so the temperature ratio is 13.6x (10.2 K against 0.75 K), and the
thermo-optic path coefficient chi = dn/dT + (n - 1)(1 + nu) alpha is ~1.8x larger for
sapphire (with its dn/dT still `[VALUE?]`).
Third, the proposal's footnote 1 scaling, dT ~ P_abs / (kappa w), holds for a
half-space; a 0.5 to 1 mm wafer under a 20 mm beam is in the opposite (thin-plate)
limit, where dT ~ P_abs / (4 pi kappa t) [1 + 2 ln(R/a)], nearly independent of beam
size at fixed absorbed power. Both limits are reproduced by the solver
(`tests/test_thermal.py`). The qualitative argument of Section 3 of the proposal (heating
grows with beam size at fixed intensity) survives, and is stronger in the plate limit,
since absorbed power grows as w^2 at fixed intensity.

Implication for the ref. 4 shortfall (12.2 W measured against 15.0 W predicted). We
model ref. 4 with its stated geometry (2.5 mm air, 3.5 mm Pyrex, 9 mm vacuum, NA 0.58,
850 nm) and a Gaussian beam whose 1/e^2 diameter equals the aperture (w = R, which
reproduces its 86.5 % truncated transmission); its substrate is not stated, so we assume
1 mm fused silica `[VALUE?]`. If the 19 % deficit were a Strehl loss of the refocused
spots, it would need a_film = 22 % and a 560 K temperature rise, which is not plausible.
As a fixed-plane loss (atoms held at the cold focal plane while the focus moves) it
needs a_film = 1.16 %, a 29 K rise and a -1.18 um focal shift. For a 1.15 um Si-rich
film at 850 nm, 1.16 % absorption corresponds to an extinction coefficient of order
1.4e-3 [DERIVED: k ~ a lambda / (4 pi h f), fill fraction f ~ 0.5], well above typical
SiN values `[VALUE?: measured k of CNNP films]`. With the same a_film, the ref. 14 case
(2.5 W on 5 mm, w/R = 1.44) has a fixed-plane Strehl of 0.998, consistent with its
unchanged efficiency. Hence substrate heating explains the ref. 4 deficit only if the film
absorbs at the per-cent level; at 0.01 to 0.1 % it is negligible. We therefore regard the
thermal explanation in the proposal as Speculative. The model points to two inexpensive,
discriminating measurements for H2a: the film absorption itself (photothermal or
calorimetric, at 1075 nm), and the focal-plane position against power. The model predicts
the focus moves by -0.18 um per 100 W per 0.1 % absorption on 0.5 mm fused silica with
a top-hat beam.
Thermo-optic changes inside the pillars are negligible by comparison: for LPCVD SiN
(dn/dT ~ 2.5e-5 /K `[VALUE?]`) at dT = 10 K, dn = 2.5e-4 changes the pillar phase by
~2 mrad (DERIVED, dphi/dn ~ k0 h ~ 9 rad); even the Si-rich value of 1.65e-4 /K
(Nejadriahi et al., Opt. Express 2020, DOI 10.1364/OE.396969) gives ~15 mrad.

## 6. Limitations

The results are model-dependent in the following specific ways.

(i) Scalar optics. All propagation is scalar. At NA 0.5 the scalar normalisation is
ambiguous at the 5 % level: the pupil model, which conserves the z-component of the
Poynting flux, predicts a peak intensity per watt 5 % above the full-pixel scalar
angular-spectrum result, which conserves the integral of |U|^2. A linearly polarised
focus at NA 0.55 is also slightly elliptical, and its depth differs from the scalar value
by a few per cent `[VALUE?: quantify with a vector Debye-Wolf model]`. Absolute trap depths
therefore carry a ~5 to 10 % model uncertainty. Relative quantities (uniformity, Strehl
ratio, field dependence) are less affected.

(ii) Local-periodic approximation. The full-pixel validation used an ideal phase-only
metasurface. Meta-atom amplitude and phase errors enter through the 16-level LPA
efficiency (0.96) and the aperture-averaged supercell factor (0.886 for Si-rich SiN),
treated as multiplicative factors. Their effect on uniformity was not simulated. They add
a diffuse background and site-to-site noise that a full-wave or library-rendered
full-pixel run would quantify (the renderer accepts a lookup table; not yet run).

(iii) Supercell factor below 13 deg. Supercells were computed down to sin(theta) = 0.22
(LPCVD) and 0.27 (Si-rich); the ratio is interpolated to 1 at normal incidence. If the
inter-pillar coupling loss persists at small deflections, the factor is lower by up to
~0.05 (Provisional). The supercell results themselves are converged to 0.4 % in Fourier
order and grid.

(iv) The full-pixel validation is limited to a 5 mm aperture by memory (10^8 pixels,
~5 GB). The 18 to 20 mm in-cell design is evaluated only with the pupil model and the
ray-traced aberration model, which the 5 mm case validates for field dependence but not
for the edge-generated zero order (Section 4.3). Block-wise propagation of the full
aperture, as in refs. 4 and 14, is the next numerical step.

(v) Thermal model. Steady state, rim held at ambient, linear face losses, photo-elastic
terms neglected. The substrate of ref. 4, the dn/dT of sapphire and of our films, and
the film absorption are not known and are marked `[VALUE?]`.

(vi) Fabrication. Proximity effects, sidewall angle and stitching errors of electron-beam
writing are not modelled beyond a uniform width bias, a height offset and an index
error.

## 7. Next steps

1. Measure n and k of the CNNP ICP-PECVD and CoE-CPPICS LPCVD films at 1075 nm
   (ellipsometry for n, photothermal or calorimetric for k), load them through
   `materials.from_csv`, and rebuild the library. The index is the dominant
   tolerance (Section 3) and k sets the thermal margin (Section 5).
2. Run the 5 mm full-pixel validation with the library lookup instead of ideal phases,
   and with Monte Carlo width errors, to quantify meta-atom-induced non-uniformity
   and stray light.
3. Block-wise full-aperture propagation for the 18 to 20 mm in-cell design, to check the
   zero order and the edge-zone efficiency at full scale.
4. Vector Debye-Wolf focusing at NA 0.55 for absolute depths and spot ellipticity.
5. Second campaign: the dispersion-engineered library at 852/895/1064 nm (the
   single-pillar library at these wavelengths is being completed in data/library_main.h5)
   and the doublet meta-objective (B2), reusing the propagation, exact-kernel and
   aberration code.
6. Confirm the placeholders below with the PI and the fabrication centres.
